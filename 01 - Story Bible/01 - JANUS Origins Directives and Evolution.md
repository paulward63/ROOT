# PART ONE — ORIGINS

## 2. The Original Problem

Around 1999–2001, governments, universities and infrastructure
specialists are becoming increasingly concerned about society's
growing dependence upon interconnected computer systems.

Banking, telecommunications, transport, government services,
utilities and other critical systems are becoming increasingly
dependent upon networks that were never originally designed to
support civilisation on this scale.

A small British-led research programme begins investigating a
fundamental question:

> How can essential digital infrastructure continue operating when
> parts of the network on which it depends are damaged, disconnected
> or destroyed?

The objective is resilience.

It is not surveillance.

It is not artificial intelligence.

It is not autonomous government.

It is not intended to replace human control.

The original JANUS project is a distributed-computing resilience
experiment.

---

## 3. The JANUS Name

### J.A.N.U.S.

**Joint Autonomous Network for Universal Survivability**

NOTE: The expansion of the acronym remains provisional and may be
refined later. "Survivability" is the important concept and should
remain central to the name.

The name also deliberately evokes Janus, the Roman figure associated
with beginnings, endings, transitions and looking simultaneously
backward and forward.

This eventually becomes thematically significant.
